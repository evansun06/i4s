## Final recommendation

Recommendation: **do not accept a permanent EM move by Friday. Offer a 90-day acting EM trial only if your director documents the role, backs you publicly, and makes the return path real. If they won’t, decline or ask to revisit later.**

Why: you are curious about management, but this is not just “less coding.” You would become responsible for performance, conflict, priorities, pay/comp input, psychological safety, and team trust. The company says you can switch back within a year, but nobody has done it, so reversibility is unproven. Also, two teammates have more tenure and one wanted the role, so legitimacy and resentment are immediate risks.

The strongest objection is that this could be a high-leverage opportunity: managing six engineers may let you create outcomes you cannot create alone. That is real, and it is why the panel does **not** recommend an outright no. But the opportunity only works if authority matches accountability and leadership owns the transition.

Tell your director something like:

> “I’m interested, but I can say yes by Friday only to a 90-day acting EM trial, not an undefined permanent switch. I’d need a written charter covering 30/60/90 success criteria, decision rights, coding expectations, weekly support from you, and my authority around performance, escalation, and project ownership.  
>   
> I also need a real IC return path: protected level and comp, reporting line, timing, who owns the headcount, and what happens if there’s no matching IC opening.  
>   
> Before I speak with the team, I need you to talk to the senior engineer who wanted the role, explain the decision, define their growth path, and then publicly introduce my mandate and your backing.”

If they agree, run the trial deliberately. If they stay vague, assume the move is effectively one-way.
