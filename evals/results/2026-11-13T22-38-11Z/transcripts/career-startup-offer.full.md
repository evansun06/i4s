## Final recommendation

Decline the offer as-is.

You have a strong current role at $165k with good benefits, 8 months of expenses saved, no dependents, and a concrete goal to buy a condo in two years. This startup offer gives you a $25k base pay cut, higher job risk, likely higher workload, and equity that should be treated as $0 for your condo plan unless it becomes liquid. With only 14 months of runway, the company may be fundraising, cutting burn, or under pressure well before your condo timeline.

The strongest argument for taking it is career upside: being the second backend hire at a 12-person fintech could mean major ownership, architecture influence, and faster growth. That matters, but it does not outweigh the current facts unless the opportunity is unusually strong. Here, no salary movement plus seed-stage risk means you would be financing their upside with your own savings and housing timeline.

What would change the advice: base salary near your current $165k, materially better equity with clear written terms, strong traction and funding path, humane on-call expectations, excellent references from current engineers, or your explicit willingness to delay the condo.

1. Make one final counter: “I’m excited, but given my condo goal, I can’t take a $25k base cut for seed-stage risk. I’d need $165k base or materially improved equity/terms.”
2. Ask for written equity details: fully diluted %, strike price, vesting, cliff, exercise window, and dilution expectations.
