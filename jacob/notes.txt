Why not LangGraph / LangChain

LangGraph models a workflow as a graph: nodes are steps, edges decide what runs next, and shared state flows through. That's a good fit for pipelines and tool-calling agents. Our room is a different shape:

1. Cycles are short, and a plain loop fits them. A cycle is: 4 bids in parallel → take queued agents in order → stop. In our code, that's one while loop and Promise.race. As a graph, the same thing needs a supervisor node, conditional edges, and a checkpointer. Same behavior, more pieces.
2. Pausing has to be exact. AGENTS.md says no new model calls when paused or awaiting the user, background calls included, plus a hard usage budget. In our code, every call passes one gate (#acquireCall), so it's easy to see that the rule holds. With LangGraph, you'd have to trust its interrupt and resume behavior to match that rule.
3. Its chat model is the wrong shape. LangChain assumes one assistant and one user taking turns. We have four speakers, private bids, and "here's what you missed since you raised your hand." We'd end up writing our own prompts around its message classes anyway.
4. Provider freedom is already solved. LangChain's biggest draw is swapping models. Our ModelProvider interface is about 10 lines, and one adapter already covers Gemini, OpenAI, OpenRouter and local models.
5. Cost, speed and upkeep. LangChain packages are large and change often, and each layer adds some overhead per call. AGENTS.md also says not to add a dependency without a concrete need, and so far there isn't one.

When it would make sense: if agents later need multi-step tool use (search → read → summarize → search again), long-running jobs that survive a restart, or tracing in LangSmith. Even then, I'd use it only inside a single agent's research step and keep the room's queue in our own code.
